Applying, referrals and timing offers

Run a 30-company search that gets referrals, stays at five applications a week, and lands offers in the same fortnight.

Cadence: ~5 applications / week Pipeline: 25–35 companies over 6–8 weeks Notice: 60–90 days typical

  • ~30 companies in tiers; warm up on Tier 3, finish on Tier 1.

Go deeper: the strategy in full

Do not apply everywhere at once and do not apply to your dream company first. Start with Tier 3 companies to warm up (weeks 1–3 of applying), move to Tier 2, and schedule Tier 1 loops so their final rounds fall in the same fortnight. Use referrals for every company where you can find one; a referral typically moves you from a pile of hundreds to a recruiter's shortlist. Keep a tracker and treat the search like a sprint with a weekly review.

Why "Zomato (referral only)"

Some companies rarely pick from cold applications for experienced backend roles; Zomato is the commonly cited example. For these, do not waste an application without a referral. Find one or skip until you have one.

Pick 8–10 from Tier 1, 10–12 from Tier 2, 6–8 from Tier 3 and a handful of AI roles. That is roughly 30 companies, enough for 3–5 offers if you convert in line with typical funnels.

03Getting referrals

  1. 1st degreeEx-colleagues, college seniors, people you interviewed
  2. Job IDPick the exact requisition before asking; "any role" gets ignored
  3. 30 secondsSend link + resume PDF + a 3-line paste-ready blurb
  4. 2nd degreeIntro via a mutual, or a specific connection point
  5. HMFor Tier 1/2, message the team's engineering manager
  6. Follow upOnce after 5–7 days, then move on; thank people

Most product companies pay a referral bonus, so you are offering a likely-qualified candidate, not begging a favour.

Go deeper: each referral step in detail
  1. First-degree network first. Ex-colleagues, college seniors, people you have interviewed. Search LinkedIn for "Company + Software Engineer", filter by your college or past companies.
  2. Pick the specific job ID before you ask. Referrers need the requisition link; "any role" requests get ignored.
  3. Make it a 30-second task for them. Send the job link, your resume PDF, and a 3-line summary they can paste into the referral form.
  4. Second-degree. Ask a mutual contact for an intro, or message someone directly with a specific connection point (same college, same tech, a talk they gave).
  5. Hiring managers and recruiters. For Tier 1/2, a short message to the engineering manager of the team posting the role often works better than a random referral.
  6. Follow up once after 5–7 days, then move on. Thank people when you get an interview, and when you get an offer.
Referral bonuses help you

Most product companies pay employees a referral bonus. You are not asking a favour so much as offering a likely-qualified candidate. Say so implicitly by making yourself easy to refer.

06Weekly cadence

MondayPick 5 roles from the target list. Check LeetCode Discuss for each company's recent process.
TuesdaySend referral requests (2–3 people per company). Reorder resume sections per role (payments vs AI vs platform).
Wed–ThuApply with the referral or directly after 48 hours of no reply. Respond to all recruiter messages.
FridayFollow-ups for anything older than 7 days. Update tracker statuses.
Sunday30-minute review: conversion by stage, which rounds you are failing, adjust prep for next week.

"Quality" = tailored resume ordering + a referral attempt + a tracker entry. Five a week keeps the pipeline full without eating prep time.

Go deeper: the timing rules in full

Competing offers are your strongest negotiation lever, but only if they exist at the same time. Offers in India often come with 3–7 day acceptance windows, and some companies withdraw if you take too long.

  • Know each process length. Tier 3 startups: 1–3 weeks. Indian product companies: 2–5 weeks. Amazon/Uber/Atlassian/Google: 4–8 weeks including team matching.
  • Start long processes first once you are ready, short ones later. Apply to Tier 1 about 3 weeks before Tier 2 if you want them to finish together.
  • Ask recruiters to slow down or speed up. "I'd like to do the onsite in two weeks so I'm fully prepared" is normal. "I have an offer deadline on the 20th; can we finish the loop before then?" usually works for fast-movers.
  • Stack your final rounds within one or two weeks, so that offers arrive within about 10 days of each other.
  • Never accept and keep interviewing with the plan to renege; Indian product-company recruiters talk to each other, and some share information about offer dropouts.

09Notice period and buyout

1. Early release try first

Shorter last day in exchange for a credible handover: "I'll hand over all three services within 30 days."

2. Buyout

Pay basic (or gross) for unserved days; ask the new employer to cover it as a joining bonus.

3. Leave adjustment

Some contracts let earned leave offset notice days.

4. Resign before offers

More calls, but no leverage and more risk. Only with savings and strong form.

Notice is contractual: 60–90 days is typical at 3+ years; urgent hirers prefer 30–45. Resign only after a signed offer letter, ideally once background verification has started, and keep release terms in writing.

Script: asking the new employer to cover a buyout

"I'm excited to join. My contract has a 90-day notice. I can get released at 45 days
if I buy out the remaining 45, which is about ₹[X]L. Could you cover that as a
one-time joining bonus so I can start on [date] instead of [date]?"
Go deeper: notice period options in full

Notice periods in India are contractual, not set by a single national law. At 3+ years of experience 60–90 days is typical, and many companies still enforce 90. Product companies hiring urgently prefer candidates who can join within 30–45 days.

Option 1: early release

Ask your manager for a shorter last day in exchange for a thorough handover. Frame it as a favour, not a right: "I'll document and hand over all three services within 30 days." Often granted when the handover is credible.

Option 2: buyout

Pay basic (or gross, per contract) for the unserved days. Many Tier 1/2 product companies reimburse the buyout, often against proof and sometimes capped; ask the new employer to add it as a joining bonus line.

Option 3: leave adjustment

Some contracts allow earned leave to offset notice days. Check your policy and leave balance.

Option 4: resign before offers

Some candidates resign first to show "serving notice" on profiles. It raises call volume but removes leverage and adds risk. Only consider it with savings and strong interview form.

Timing matters: resign only after you have a signed offer letter (not a verbal one) and ideally after background verification has started. Keep written records of your release terms.

Common mistakes
  • Applying to your dream company first, cold.
  • "Refer me for any role" without a job ID.
  • Letting one fast offer expire before others arrive.
  • Resigning on a verbal offer.
  • 15 applications and under 3 calls, but sending more instead of fixing the resume.

10Questions

How many applications before I should expect interviews?

With referrals and a solid resume, a 30–50% interview rate from Tier 2/3 applications is realistic; cold applications to Tier 1 convert far lower. If you send 15 applications and get under 3 calls, fix the resume and headline before sending more.

Should I re-apply after a rejection?

Most companies have a cool-off of 6–12 months per role family. Note it in the tracker. A different team at a large company sometimes ignores the cool-off; ask the recruiter.

Is it fine to interview while my current company doesn't know?

Yes, that is normal. Schedule rounds early morning or late evening, use personal devices and email, and do not mention confidential details of your employer.

A recruiter asks for my current payslips before the interview. What do I do?

Defer politely: "Happy to share documents at the offer stage for verification. For now, I'm targeting ₹[X]–[Y]L fixed." Payslips are usually needed only at offer or background check.

11Quick check

You want Amazon and Razorpay offers in the same week. What do you do?

Tier 1 loops run 4–8 weeks including team matching; Indian product companies 2–5. Start the long one first.

Which referral request is most likely to work?

Make it a 30-second task for the referrer; they need the exact requisition.

15 applications sent, 2 calls. What next?

Under 3 calls from 15 means the resume or profile is the bottleneck, not volume.

When should you resign from your current job?

Verbal offers can be withdrawn. Keep release terms in writing.
