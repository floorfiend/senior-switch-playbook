Payment Service

Design a payment service that never charges a user twice and never loses money. It must stay correct even when the bank does not reply in time.

Time-box: 45–60 min (Angel One ran 1 h+) Seen: Angel One, Paytm ×2, Kotak, PhonePe, Slice, Rippling, 2025–26
Angel OnePaytmPhonePeKotakSliceRipplingRazorpay
In 30 seconds
  • Every hop needs its own "this is the same request" key: app to you, and you to the payment gateway.
  • If the bank does not reply in time, the payment is PENDING (unknown). It is never FAILED.
  • Save the event in an outbox table in the same transaction. Do not "commit, then publish to Kafka".
  • A double-entry ledger plus a daily check against the bank's file catch everything else.
Picture it

You pay at a toll plaza with FASTag, and the screen freezes. Did it deduct? You do not pay again in cash. You wait for the SMS or check your statement. A payment service works the same way. "Unknown" is a real state, and it gets resolved later.

Words you'll see
PSP
Payment service provider, a gateway like Razorpay or Juspay. It talks to banks and UPI for you.
Idempotency key
A unique id the caller sends with a request. If the same id arrives twice, we do the work once. Pressing "Pay" twice should charge you once.
Webhook
The PSP calling our server to say "payment X succeeded". It is how we hear results that arrive later.
Outbox
A table where we save "events to publish", in the same transaction as the payment. A separate process then sends them to Kafka.
Double-entry ledger
An accounting log where every money movement is written as two or more lines that add up to zero. Money is never created or lost.
Reconciliation
A daily job that compares our records with the bank's settlement file, line by line, and fixes any differences.
Circuit breaker
A switch that stops sending traffic to a partner that keeps failing, and tries again later.
Terminal state
A final status, like SUCCEEDED or FAILED. A payment never moves out of it.

01What the interviewer is grading

Idempotency end to end

The app calls your API, and your API calls the PSP. Each of these hops needs its own idempotency key.

UNKNOWN / PENDING state

Treating a timeout as a failure is the classic bug. The user pays again, and gets charged twice.

DB + Kafka atomicity

"Commit, then publish" can lose events. A transactional outbox fixes this.

Money correctness

Use a double-entry ledger. Never edit entries. Calculate balances from entries instead of overwriting them.

Reconciliation

The daily check that catches anything every other part missed.

Partner failure

Retry only where it is safe. Use circuit breakers. Route payments across several PSPs.

Go deeper: what the reports said

The Angel One SDE-2 round (Sep 2026, candidate selected) ran for over an hour. It went deep on database commits and transaction handling, Kafka streams, partner bank failures, vendor payment flow, and payment failures and retries. Paytm asked for a Shiprocket-like system, meaning order management plus payments. Paytm also asked for a risk management service, with a focus on the database. Kotak's screening asked "what is an idempotency key and how would you use it for placing an order". PhonePe probes double payment. Slice asks about wallet consistency. Rippling asks about ingesting metrics without double counting.

02Clarify and scope

Functional

  • Create a payment for an order, and send the user to the PSP (redirect or UPI intent)
  • Learn the result from a webhook and from status checks
  • Full and partial refunds, and payouts to vendors
  • Payment history, ledger, daily reconciliation

Non-functional

  • Zero double charges and zero lost payments
  • Create is up 99.99% of the time; 99 in 100 calls under 300 ms, not counting the PSP
  • Every rupee can be traced; the audit log is never edited
  • Never store card data (PCI DSS card rules; the PSP keeps it). Keep data in India (RBI rule).

Out of scope

  • Building a card network or UPI switch
  • Full fraud ML (the risk check is just a hook)
  • Tax and invoicing
Go deeper: five questions to ask first
  • Are we a merchant that uses PSPs (Razorpay, Juspay, Stripe)? Or are we the PSP, talking to banks and NPCI? Most rounds mean the first. That is an internal payment service for an app, like a broker or a shop.
  • Which payment methods? UPI, cards (with a 3DS bank-verification redirect), netbanking and wallet. UPI is asynchronous: the result comes later. That alone forces a PENDING state.
  • Which flows? Pay-in (the user pays), refunds, payouts to vendors (Angel One's "vendor payment flow") and wallet top-up.
  • Authorise and capture separately? Cards can hold money first and take it later. Online shops often take it only when the item ships.
  • How consistent? Money movements must be strongly consistent and auditable. Read screens, like payment history, can lag a little.

Go deeper: key scoping, data model and each component

The caller creates the key. We store it per caller (merchant_id + key), so two merchants can use the same key safely. Build it from the business intent. For an order, that is orderId plus the attempt number. A random UUID per click is useless when a user taps "Pay" twice. The order id is what makes the second tap do nothing (PhonePe's double-payment question).

Use Postgres (or MySQL) for the money path. You need ACID transactions across the payment, ledger and outbox rows. ACID means all of them save together or none do. You also need unique constraints for idempotency, and row locks. When one primary is not enough, shard by merchant_id or user_id, with Citus or in the app. Kafka carries events to the order, notification and analytics services. It is never the source of truth for balances. Amounts are whole numbers in the smallest unit (paise).

  • Payment service owns the state machine. Every status change goes through one method with a conditional update on (status, version).
  • Risk check (the Paytm risk-management variant) runs before calling the PSP, and we wait for it. It uses speed counters in Redis: payments per user per hour, per device and per card. It adds a rule engine and an ML score. It answers ALLOW, REVIEW or BLOCK within a hard time limit. If it is down, small amounts go through and large amounts are blocked.
  • PSP router picks a gateway by payment method, success rate over the last 5 minutes, and cost. A circuit breaker per PSP (Resilience4j) takes a struggling bank out of rotation.
  • Webhook receiver checks the HMAC signature, which proves the message really came from the PSP. It saves the raw event and replies 200 OK within milliseconds. The real processing happens later. PSPs resend webhooks for hours, so it drops repeats by the PSP's event id.
  • Status poller asks the PSP about payments stuck in PENDING for too long, for example at 2, 5, 15 and 60 minutes.
  • Reconciliation loads the settlement files the next day (T+1) and compares them line by line.

06Deep dives

6.1 Idempotency done properly

  1. ClaimTry to save the key as IN_PROGRESS. If the save works, this request owns it. If the key already exists, read that row (INSERT … 'IN_PROGRESS' ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING).
  2. ReplayFinished with the same request body: return the saved response. Different body: reject (HTTP 422). Still running: "try later" (HTTP 409). Running but stuck after a crash: take over.
  3. Phase 1Create the payment as CREATED and commit.
  4. Phase 2Call the PSP, sending our payment id as its idempotency key.
  5. Phase 3In one transaction, save the result, ledger lines, outbox event and the final response on the key row.

Key in the DB, same transaction usual pick

  • Stays correct when servers crash
  • One extra write per request

Default for money.

Key in Redis (SETNX)

  • Fast and cheap
  • Not saved together with the DB commit; lost if Redis evicts it

Duplicate checks where money is not involved (metrics, notifications).

Go deeper: the full idempotency algorithm
  1. Run INSERT INTO idempotency_keys (scope, key, request_hash, status) VALUES (..., 'IN_PROGRESS') ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING. If one row was inserted, you own the request. If zero rows, read the existing row.
  2. If it exists and is DONE with the same request hash, return the saved response. A different hash returns 422. IN_PROGRESS returns 409, or you wait briefly. If locked_at is old, the owner crashed, so take over.
  3. Do the work. Save the payment row, ledger lines, outbox row and final response on the key row in one transaction.
  4. The PSP call cannot be inside that transaction. So split the request into atomic phases, each with a recovery point. Phase 1 creates the payment (CREATED) and commits. Phase 2 calls the PSP, with our payment id as the PSP's idempotency key or merchant reference. Phase 3 records the result. A retry continues from the last committed phase. This is Stripe's (Brandur's) recovery-point pattern. Airbnb's Orpheus uses the same "pre-RPC, RPC, post-RPC" split.

Keep the key row for 24 h to 7 days. Store it in the same database as the payment, so the check and the write share one transaction. With a Redis-only check, there is a gap. Redis can say "new" while the database has already committed the payment.

6.2 The payment state machine

Legal moves only; terminal states never go back
Show Java transition method
// single place where status changes; webhook, poller and recon all call this
@Transactional
public boolean transition(String paymentId, Status from, Status to, Source src, String payload) {
    int rows = jdbc.update("""
        UPDATE payments SET status = ?, version = version + 1, updated_at = now()
        WHERE payment_id = ? AND status = ?""", to.name(), paymentId, from.name());
    if (rows == 0) return false;                 // someone else moved it: idempotent no-op
    events.append(paymentId, from, to, src, payload);
    if (to == Status.SUCCEEDED) ledger.postCapture(paymentId);   // same txn
    outbox.add(paymentId, "PAYMENT_" + to.name(), payload);       // same txn
    return true;
}
Go deeper: the UNKNOWN problem in full

Note on the figure: PROCESSING and PENDING can also move to FAILED. Final states never move backwards.

When the bank call times out, you do not know whether money moved. Marking it FAILED and letting the user retry is how double payments happen. The first debit succeeds at the bank, and the retry debits again. Instead, move to PENDING and show "payment processing". Settle it from the webhook, the status poller or reconciliation. Retry the PSP call only with the same merchant reference, so the PSP can spot the repeat. If the user insists on paying again, create attempt 2 on the same order. Then refund whichever attempt succeeds second. We check "order already paid" each time a payment succeeds.

The webhook and the poller can race to report success. The conditional update lets exactly one of them win. Events in the wrong order, like a FAILED webhook after SUCCEEDED, are rejected by the table of allowed moves.

Go deeper: outbox and sagas

"Update the payment, then publish to Kafka" breaks in both orders. Commit, then crash before publishing, and the event is lost, so the order never ships. Publish, then roll back, and you announce a payment that does not exist. Instead, write the event to an outbox table in the same transaction. Then Debezium or a poller publishes it to Kafka. Debezium does CDC (change data capture): it reads the database's write-ahead log (WAL), where every change is recorded. Delivery is at-least-once, so readers drop repeats by event id. Kafka transactions do not help, because they cannot include Postgres. For the Shiprocket-style order and payment flow, the order service reads PAYMENT_SUCCEEDED and moves the order forward. If the order cannot be fulfilled, it sends a refund command. This is a saga: a chain of steps where each step has an "undo" action. It is not a 2PC (two-phase commit, where all systems lock and commit together).

6.4 Double-entry ledger and wallet consistency

Every money movement is written as at least two lines that add up to zero. Here is a ₹499 UPI payment with a ₹5 gateway fee.

AccountDebitCredit
PSP_RECEIVABLE₹494
GATEWAY_FEES (expense)₹5
MERCHANT_PAYABLE / revenue₹499

Immutable

Never edit an entry. To fix a mistake, add a new entry that reverses it.

Wallet debit

Take money only if the balance is enough, in one statement, together with the ledger line. It cannot overdraw: UPDATE … SET balance = balance - ? WHERE … AND balance >= ?.

Hot accounts

A merchant receiving thousands of payments per second locks one row. Split it into sub-accounts or group credits into batches.

Go deeper: ledger details

Entries are never edited. Fixes are new entries that reverse the old ones. A balance is the sum of its entries. It is cached on the account row and updated in the same transaction. For a wallet (Slice), a debit runs UPDATE accounts SET balance = balance - ? , version = version + 1 WHERE account_id = ? AND balance >= ?. This is one atomic statement that cannot overdraw. The ledger insert happens in the same transaction. Some accounts are very busy, like a merchant receiving thousands per second. Every write locks that one row. So split the account into sub-accounts, or group credits into batches. Uber's payments platform follows the same "always adds up to zero" rule. It stores ledgers in a store that never edits records (LedgerStore).

6.5 Reconciliation

PSP file saysWe sayAction
successsuccessMATCHED: nothing to do
successPENDING / FAILEDMoney was taken. Move to SUCCEEDED, or refund automatically if the order was cancelled.
no recordsuccessHigh severity: investigate before paying the merchant
₹494₹499Amount mismatch: send to the finance team's queue

Every morning, take yesterday's (T−1) settlement file from the gateway. Match each line by psp_ref and amount. Every other safeguard can fail, but this check still catches the problem within a day.

Go deeper: reconciliation in full

Every morning, download the PSP or bank settlement file for the previous day (T−1). Match it against payments by psp_ref and amount. There are four outcomes.

  • MATCHED: nothing to do.
  • PSP says success, we say PENDING or FAILED: money was taken. Move to SUCCEEDED and fulfil the order. Or refund automatically if the order was cancelled.
  • We say success, PSP has no record: high severity. Investigate before paying out to the merchant.
  • Amount mismatch: a fee or partial-capture problem. Send it to the finance ops queue.

Reconciliation is why the design may lag behind the outside world for a while (eventual consistency). Every safeguard before it can fail, and reconciliation still catches it within a day.

08Scaling, failure handling and consistency

Partner bank down

Use a circuit breaker per gateway, and send new payments elsewhere. Never move a payment that is already in progress. That can debit the user twice.

Retry policy

Retry only safe-to-repeat calls. Wait longer each time, with some randomness (jitter). Stop after a limit, park the message in a DLQ (a holding queue for failures) and alert. Never retry business errors like "insufficient funds".

Timeouts

Give up on the gateway before the user's screen gives up (10 s vs 30 s). A timeout means PENDING.

Database

Keep a live copy in another data centre that confirms every write (RPO 0, no data loss). Split tables by month and archive old months.

10× / 100×

Shard by merchant or user. For money, one region works and one stands by. Reads can be served from both.

Go deeper: full scaling notes
  • Partner bank down (Angel One). Put a circuit breaker on each PSP or bank, based on success rate. The router sends new payments to a backup PSP. Payments already in progress at the failed PSP stay PENDING. They are settled by polling or reconciliation. Never re-route an in-progress payment to another PSP, because that can debit twice.
  • Retry policy. Retry only calls that are safe to repeat, or calls that carry a PSP idempotency key. Wait longer after each try (exponential backoff), with random jitter. Cap the number of tries. Then park the message in a DLQ topic (dead-letter queue, a separate parking area for messages that keep failing) and alert. Do not retry 4xx business errors like insufficient funds.
  • Timeouts. Set the PSP client timeout below the user-facing timeout, for example 10 s vs 30 s. Treat a timeout as PENDING.
  • Webhook storms. The receiver only saves and queues. Readers scale out. Kafka key = payment_id keeps events for one payment in order.
  • Database. Run a primary with a synchronous copy in another availability zone (AZ). RPO 0 means no money data is lost if the primary dies. Add an asynchronous copy for reads. Split payments and ledger_entries into monthly partitions, and archive older ones.
  • 10× / 100×. Shard by merchant or user. Split busy ledger accounts. Run one outbox relay per shard. For the money path, use active-passive regions: one region writes, one stands by. Avoid two regions writing the same balance. Read APIs can be active-active.
  • Idempotent metrics (Rippling). Each event carries an id. The aggregator keeps a set of seen ids per window, or upserts by event id. Then a re-sent event is not counted twice.

09Mistakes that got candidates rejected

Rejected for this
  • Treating a gateway timeout as a failure, and letting the user try again.
  • "Save to DB, then publish to Kafka" with no outbox. Or calling the PSP inside a database transaction that holds row locks for seconds.
  • Idempotency described as "check Redis if we saw this request". No request hash, no in-progress state, and no link to the database commit.
  • Using floats for money. Or one balance column with no ledger.
  • No reconciliation, or not knowing what a settlement file is.
  • Retrying everything, including bank calls that are unsafe to repeat. Or moving an in-progress payment to a second PSP.

10Quick check

The bank call gets no reply after 10 s. What status do you set?

You do not know if the money moved. FAILED plus a retry is the classic double charge.

Which idempotency key best stops a double tap on "Pay"?

The key must stand for what the user is trying to do. A new UUID per click makes the second tap look like a new payment.

You commit the payment, but Kafka is down. With an outbox, what happens?

The outbox row was saved together with the payment. It is delivered late, maybe twice, and readers skip repeats.

Gateway A is failing. One payment is already in progress at A. What do you do with it?

The in-progress payment may still succeed at A. Sending it to B as well can debit the user twice.

11Follow-up questions interviewers asked

The bank call timed out. What do you show the user, and what does the system do?

Show "processing, we will confirm shortly". Mark it PENDING, never FAILED. Three things can settle it. The webhook, status polls at 2, 5, 15 and 60 minutes, and reconciliation the next day (T+1). If it finally fails, release the order. If it succeeds after the user already paid again, refund the duplicate.

How exactly do you prevent a double payment when the user taps Pay twice?

The app sends the same idempotency key, built from the order id and attempt. The unique constraint on (scope, key) makes the second request read the first one's state. There is a second guard too. When marking a payment SUCCEEDED, we check that the order has no other successful payment (unique order_id). That catches duplicates from different attempts and triggers an automatic refund.

You commit to the DB, and then Kafka is down. What happens?

Nothing is lost. The event sits in the outbox table, saved in the same commit. The relay keeps retrying until Kafka recovers. Readers see the event late, maybe twice, and skip repeats by event id.

How do you design the idempotency key for a food-delivery order (Kotak)?

The app creates it once per user intent. Use userId + cartId + attempt, or a UUID made when the checkout screen opens and reused on retries. The server stores it per user, along with a hash of the request. On a repeat, it returns the original response. Keys expire after 24 h.

Two webhooks arrive in the wrong order: FAILED after SUCCEEDED.

The table of allowed moves forbids leaving a final state. So the conditional update changes zero rows, and we log the event for audit. If the PSP really reversed the payment, that comes as a separate refund or chargeback event, with its own flow.

How do you keep a wallet balance correct when two debits happen at once?

Use a single conditional update (balance >= amount), with the ledger insert in the same transaction. Or lock the account row first with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE. Optimistic version checks also work when collisions are rare. Never read the balance, change it in app code, and write it back without a lock.

What does reconciliation catch that webhooks and polling do not?

Webhooks lost after the PSP stopped retrying. Bugs in our own status handling. Reversals on the PSP side. Fee mismatches. Settled amounts that differ from authorised amounts. It is the only check against the bank's own books.

13Cheat sheet

Cheat sheet
  • Idempotency key = orderId + attempt. Store a unique (scope, key) row in the same database.
  • Three phases: create and commit, call the PSP with our id, save the result in one transaction.
  • Statuses: CREATED, PROCESSING, PENDING, then SUCCEEDED or FAILED, then REFUNDED. Final states never move back.
  • Timeout means PENDING. Settle it by webhook, polling (2/5/15/60 min) or reconciliation.
  • One transition() method with a conditional update. Races then change nothing.
  • Outbox plus Debezium. Readers skip repeats by event id. Use a saga, not 2PC.
  • Double-entry, whole paise, entries never edited, balance only a cache.
  • Circuit breaker per PSP. Never re-route a payment already in progress.
