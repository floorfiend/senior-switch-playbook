Negotiating SDE-2 / Senior offers in India

How to decode an Indian "CTC", compare offers on base first, anchor on market rather than your current pay, use competing offers, resist downleveling, get your notice buyout covered, and value startup ESOPs honestly. Written for a move from ₹21L to a ₹32–40L fixed target, with a stretch of ₹55–85L year-one total at top-tier companies.

01Decoding an Indian CTC

"CTC" (cost to company) is everything the employer might spend on you, which makes it the least useful number for comparing offers. Break every offer into these lines:

ComponentWhat it isHow to value it
Base / fixed salaryGuaranteed annual pay, monthly in-hand after tax and PF100%. The anchor for all future hikes and the next switch.
RetiralsEmployer PF (12% of basic, often capped), gratuity (~4.8% of basic)PF ≈ real savings; gratuity only after ~5 years. Some offers fold these into "fixed"; ask.
Variable / performance bonusTarget % of base (10–20% is common), paid annually or half-yearlyValue at the historical payout rate (ask: "what was the average payout last two cycles?"), not 100%.
Joining / sign-on bonusOne-time cash, often with a 12-month clawback if you leaveYear-1 only. Useful to cover buyout or lost bonus; ignore for long-term comparison.
Retention / second-year bonusCash in year 2, sometimes to smooth back-loaded vestingReal but time-bound; check clawback terms.
RSUs (listed companies)Shares granted in USD/INR value, vesting over ~4 yearsGrant ÷ 4 per year if evenly vested, adjusted for the schedule; currency and stock-price risk.
ESOPs (private companies)Options to buy shares at a strike priceHeavy discount; see section 10.
BenefitsInsurance, meal cards, NPS, learning budget, relocationSmall; occasionally inflated into CTC. Strip out.
CTC inflation tricks

Insurance premiums, gratuity, "flexi" components, the full 4-year ESOP value counted in year 1, and joining bonus counted in annual CTC. Always ask for the offer as: fixed, target variable, joining bonus, equity (units, grant value, vesting schedule), and benefits separately.

02Equity and vesting schedules

ScheduleYear 1Year 2Year 3Year 4Seen at
Even (with 1-year cliff)25%25%25%25%Most RSU grants and Indian startup ESOPs
Back-loaded 5/15/40/405%15%40%40%Amazon (offset by year-1 and year-2 sign-on cash)
Back-loaded 10/20/30/4010%20%30%40%Some listed and late-stage companies

Back-loaded vesting makes year-1 total look smaller and makes leaving early expensive. Worked example: a $100K grant on 5/15/40/40 vests $5K in year 1 versus $25K on an even schedule. Amazon compensates with sign-on cash spread over years 1 and 2; compare year-1, year-2 and the 4-year average, not just year-1.

Also ask about refreshers (annual top-up grants). Without them, total compensation falls sharply in year 5, which is one reason people switch at year 4.

03Why compare base first

  • It is the only guaranteed number. Variable payouts get cut, stock prices fall, ESOPs may never become liquid.
  • It compounds. Annual hikes are a percentage of base, and your next employer will anchor on your base.
  • It sets your monthly life. EMIs and savings run on in-hand pay.
  • Equity is a bonus layer. Choose between two offers with similar base on equity; do not take a ₹6L lower base for "₹20L ESOPs".

Comparison order: base → guaranteed cash in year 1 (base + joining) → 4-year average total at today's stock price → upside (ESOP, growth) → non-money (team, learning, AI work, WLB, brand).

What a jump from ₹21L means

₹21L to ₹35L is a 67% hike. Indian product companies routinely make jumps like this for SDE-2 hires because they price to the band, not your current pay. HR may still say "we give 30–40% over current"; the scripts below are how you move the conversation to the band.

05"What's your current CTC / expected CTC?"

Almost every Indian recruiter asks both in the first call, and many will verify current pay via payslips at offer stage. Do not lie about current CTC; misstatement found during background verification can get offers revoked. You can, however, decline to let it anchor the offer.

When asked for current CTC

"My current fixed is ₹21L. I'd rather we price this role on the SDE-2 band rather
than my current number, because I'm moving to a larger scope and I'm interviewing
for SDE-2 at a few product companies. For this role I'm looking at ₹38–42L fixed."

When asked for expected CTC before interviews

"I'm targeting ₹38–42L fixed plus standard variable and equity, depending on level
and scope. If the band for this role is lower, I'd like to know now so we don't
waste each other's time. What's the band for this level?"

When they push: "We can only do 30% on current"

"I understand that's a common guideline. My other conversations are at ₹36L+ fixed
for the same level, so a 30% cap would put this offer well below market. If the
team sees me at SDE-2, I'd ask you to price to the band. Is there flexibility
there, or should we look at joining bonus and level instead?"
Anchor high, inside the band

State a range whose bottom is at or above your real target (target ₹35L → say ₹38–42L). The first number spoken tends to anchor the negotiation; let it be yours and let it be grounded in band data.

06Anchoring and counter-offer scripts

Receiving the verbal offer

"Thank you, I'm really excited about the team. Could you send the breakdown in
writing: fixed, variable target, joining bonus, equity units, grant value and
vesting schedule? I'll come back within two days."

Never accept or reject on the call. Ask for time and the breakdown.

Counter (no competing offer yet)

"I've looked at the breakdown. Based on the SDE-2 band at comparable companies
and the scope we discussed (owning [service area], on-call, mentoring), I was
expecting fixed closer to ₹40L. If you can get the fixed to ₹40L, I'm ready to
accept and give notice this week."

When base is capped

"If fixed is truly at the top of the band, could we close the gap with a ₹5L
joining bonus to cover my notice buyout and the bonus I'm forfeiting, and an
additional [N] RSUs?"
  • Ask for one or two specific things at a time, with a reason.
  • Tie your ask to a commitment ("I'm ready to accept") so the recruiter can sell it internally.
  • Keep it warm and in writing after calls. Recruiters negotiate for you with the hiring manager and compensation team.

07Using competing offers

Competing written offers are the strongest lever. That's why timing offers to land together matters.

"I want to be transparent: I have a written offer from [Company B] at ₹42L fixed,
₹4L joining and RSUs worth about ₹12L a year. [Your company] is my first choice
because of [team / problem / AI work]. If you can match the fixed at ₹42L, I'll
sign today."
  • Share real numbers only; many companies ask for the offer letter. Fabrication ends the process.
  • Compare like with like: a startup's ESOP is not equivalent to listed-company RSUs; say so if they try to "match" with ESOPs.
  • Tier 1 companies match Tier 1 offers more readily than they match a Tier 3 offer. Indian unicorns often respond to Tier 1 offers with ESOP top-ups.
  • Do not run an auction across five companies for ₹1L more. Two strong offers is enough.

08Resisting downleveling

At 5 YOE you will sometimes be offered SDE-1 / L3 / P30 "with fast promotion". Level matters more than any single negotiation: it sets band, scope, and your next switch.

"I appreciate the offer. I currently own [2] production services end-to-end,
lead incident response and mentor [3] engineers, which maps to SDE-2 scope.
Could you share which signals in the loop pointed to SDE-1? If it's a specific
round, I'd be happy to do an additional round to demonstrate that level."
  • Ask for the specific feedback. If it was one weak round, ask for a re-evaluation or an extra round.
  • If they hold firm, compare with your other offers on level. A downlevel at a Tier 1 can still beat SDE-2 at a Tier 3 on money, but costs you scope.
  • If accepting a lower level, negotiate to the top of that band and get the promotion timeline and criteria in writing (email from the hiring manager).

09Notice buyout and joining date

With 60–90 day notice periods common, companies often ask "can you join in 30 days?". Turn this into money: ask the new employer to cover the buyout through a joining bonus.

"I can join on [date] if I buy out 45 days of notice, about ₹[X]L. Could you add
that as a joining bonus? Otherwise my earliest date is [date + 45 days]."

Also account for what you forfeit: an annual bonus paid next month, unvested ESOPs, a retention bonus. Name the amount and ask for it as joining bonus. See Applying & referrals for early-release negotiation with your current employer.

10Evaluating startup ESOPs

Ask these questions, in writing:

  1. Number of options, strike (exercise) price, and the latest fair market value per share.
  2. Total shares outstanding (fully diluted), so you can compute your percentage.
  3. Latest preferred-share price from the last funding round, and the valuation.
  4. Vesting schedule and cliff; acceleration on acquisition.
  5. Exercise window after leaving (90 days vs several years makes a large difference).
  6. History of buybacks or liquidity events, and whether ex-employees could participate.

Valuing them

Paper value per year  = (options vesting per year) x (FMV - strike)
Realistic value       = paper value x probability of liquidity x (1 - expected dilution)

Example: 4,000 options/year, FMV ₹1,200, strike ₹200
  paper value           = 4,000 x 1,000 = ₹40L/year
  liquidity 40%, dilution 30% -> 40L x 0.4 x 0.7 ≈ ₹11L/year realistic

Tax: in India, exercising ESOPs is taxed as a perquisite on (FMV − strike) at your slab rate in the year of exercise, before you can sell; sale later attracts capital-gains tax. Eligible DPIIT-recognised startups can defer the tax deduction on the perquisite, but you still owe it eventually. Do not exercise large grants without a plan to pay that tax.

Liquidity does happen, unevenly

Indian startups have run sizeable buybacks (Swiggy's pre-IPO buyback exceeded ₹900 crore in 2022, and 23 startups ran buyback schemes in 2024). But most ESOPs at early-stage companies never become liquid. Value them at a steep discount and choose on base.

11Offer checklist

  • Breakdown in writing: fixed, variable target and historical payout, joining bonus and clawback, equity units, grant value, vesting schedule, refreshers.
  • Level and title confirmed (SDE-2 / L4 / P40 / SSE), and who you report to.
  • Year-1, year-2 and 4-year average computed on the same basis for every offer.
  • Buyout and forfeited bonus covered or explicitly declined.
  • Joining date agreed; offer letter signed before resigning.
  • Background verification documents ready (payslips, relieving letters).
  • Non-compete / moonlighting / IP clauses read.
  • Thank-you sent to recruiters and referrers, including companies you decline.
Is it risky to negotiate? Will they withdraw the offer?

Polite, specific, evidence-based negotiation almost never causes withdrawal at product companies; recruiters expect it. Withdrawals happen with ultimatums, fabricated offers, or very long delays.

How much can I ask for on top of the first offer?

Typical successful asks move fixed by 5–15% or add a joining bonus; with a strong competing offer, more. If the first offer is already at the top of the band, the room is in joining bonus and equity.

Should I tell my current company and take a counter-offer?

Usually no. Counter-offers fix pay but not the reasons you left, and you have spent goodwill. Accept one only if the new offer was mainly about money and your current role has real growth.
