A resume that gets SDE-2 / Senior backend calls

One page, one section per service you own, every bullet saying what the system does, how big it is, what you decided and what changed because of it. This lesson gives you the structure, before/after rewrites, ATS rules and the profile settings that make recruiters at Indian product companies reach out first.

01What the resume has to do

A recruiter at Razorpay, Uber or Atlassian spends well under a minute on the first pass. They are answering three questions: is this person at the right level (5 YOE, owns production systems, not just tickets), does the stack match (Java, Spring Boot, distributed systems, a queue, a relational DB, cloud), and is there evidence of impact (numbers). The hiring manager who reads it later asks a fourth: which of these bullets will I dig into in the project deep-dive? Every bullet you write is a question you will be asked. Write only what you can defend for ten minutes.

The shift from SDE-1 to SDE-2 on paper

SDE-1 resumes list tasks ("built APIs", "fixed bugs"). SDE-2 / Senior resumes show ownership: a service you are on call for, a design decision you made and why, a number that moved, and someone you helped grow. If a bullet could have been written by anyone on your team, rewrite it.

02One-page structure

With five years of experience, one page is the norm at Indian product companies. Two pages signal that you cannot prioritise. Use a single-column layout, a standard font, 10–11pt, margins around 0.5–0.7 inch.

Grouping by service is the key structural choice. "Experience → Company → Signing Workflow Service (owner) → bullets" tells the reader in one glance that you own systems, and it hands the hiring manager a ready-made agenda for the deep-dive.

03The bullet formula

Google's former head of people operations, Laszlo Bock, popularised the formula "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]". For backend engineers, extend it into four parts and make sure each owned service has at least one bullet covering each:

1. What it does

The system in business terms: "multi-tenant signing workflow service that routes documents to signers in sequence or parallel".

2. Scale

RPS, documents/day, tenants, data size, p99 latency, availability. Real numbers, rounded honestly.

3. Decisions

The technical choice and the alternative you rejected: "outbox + Kafka instead of dual writes", "Redis token bucket per tenant".

4. Impact

What moved: latency, cost, incidents, revenue unblocked, on-call pages, developer time.

Start every bullet with a strong past-tense verb that shows ownership: designed, led, owned, migrated, cut, reduced, scaled, introduced, replaced, built. Avoid worked on, helped, involved in, responsible for.

Where to find numbers when you "don't have metrics"
  • Grafana / CloudWatch / Datadog: p50/p99 latency before and after, error rate, RPS at peak.
  • AWS Cost Explorer: monthly spend of the service before and after.
  • Jira / incident tracker: number of P1/P2 incidents per quarter, MTTR.
  • Database: row counts, table size, documents per day, number of tenants.
  • CI: build time, test count, deployment frequency.

If you truly cannot measure, use scale or scope ("used by 1,200+ enterprise tenants") rather than inventing percentages. Every number will be probed.

The "we" trap on paper

"We migrated to Kubernetes" says nothing about you. If the team did it, write your slice: "Migrated [4] of the team's [11] services to EKS, wrote the shared Helm chart used by the rest". Interviewers at Blinkit, CRED and Freshworks have rejected candidates whose deep-dive revealed that the headline bullet was someone else's work.

  writes; downstream consumers (audit, webhooks, billing) became idempotent on eventId.

  signature (IT Act compliant); [180M] rows, partitioned monthly, p99 read [120ms].

Notice what this does: four service headings form the agenda for the hiring-manager round; each bullet invites a specific follow-up ("why optimistic locking over a distributed lock?", "how do you verify the hash chain?") that you can prepare for in Whiteboard your own system.

06Presenting AI work

In 2025–26, almost every product company in India asks about AI usage, and AI-engineering roles screen specifically for shipped LLM features. Two kinds of AI work belong on the resume, written differently.

AI features you built

Treat them like any backend system: what it does, model and pattern (RAG, tool use, structured output), evaluation method, cost/latency numbers, guardrails. "Eval" and "golden set" are the words that separate engineers from prompt tinkerers.

AI tools you use to build

One line at most, and only with an outcome: "Used Claude Code to migrate [40] services from Spring Boot 2 to 3 in [2] weeks; every diff reviewed and gated by contract tests." Never just "proficient in ChatGPT".

Put model and framework names in the Skills line too (Claude API, Spring AI, pgvector, MCP, LangChain) because ATS filters for AI roles search for them. Do not list anything you have only watched a video about; AI rounds go deep on evals, hallucination handling and cost. See AI-assisted coding rounds for how the same story is probed in interviews.

07ATS and keyword rules

Most Indian product companies use an applicant tracking system (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, SmartRecruiters, Darwinbox, Zoho Recruit). Recruiters search it by keyword and the parser converts your PDF into fields. Fail the parse and you are invisible.

  • Single column, no tables, no text boxes, no icons for contact details. Two-column templates often parse into a jumbled stream.
  • Export a text PDF from Google Docs, Word or LaTeX. Never a scanned image or a Canva export with text as graphics. Test: open the PDF, select all, paste into a text editor. If the order is wrong, the ATS sees the same mess.
  • Standard section headings: Experience, Skills, Education. Not "My Journey".
  • Mirror the job description's nouns: if it says "distributed systems", "event-driven", "Kafka", "system design", "microservices", make sure those exact words appear where true. Spell out once and abbreviate after: "Amazon Web Services (AWS)".
  • Dates as "Jul 2023 – Present" so tenure is computed correctly; unexplained gaps get filtered.
  • File name: Firstname_Lastname_Backend_Resume.pdf.
Keyword bank for a Java backend SDE-2

Java, Spring Boot, Spring Cloud, microservices, REST, gRPC, Kafka, RabbitMQ/SQS, PostgreSQL, MySQL, Redis, Elasticsearch, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD, distributed systems, event-driven architecture, system design, scalability, high availability, observability (Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry), multi-tenant, idempotency, caching, LLM, RAG, Claude/OpenAI API.

08LinkedIn, Naukri and Instahyre profiles

In India a large share of SDE-2 interviews start with a recruiter finding you, not you applying. Your profiles are search results; optimise them like one.

LinkedIn

  • Headline (the most searched field): "Senior Backend Engineer · Java, Spring Boot, Kafka, Distributed Systems · Building LLM features" — not "SDE at X | Ex-Y | Learner".
  • Open to Work → Recruiters only (not the green photo frame). Set titles: Software Engineer II, Senior Software Engineer, Senior Backend Engineer, SDE-2; locations: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Remote.
  • About: 4–5 lines, the summary line from your resume plus 2 achievements with numbers and one line on what you want next.
  • Experience: paste your resume bullets per service. Add skills to each role (LinkedIn's recruiter search weighs them).
  • Ask two senior colleagues for short recommendations that mention a specific system you owned.

Naukri

  • Recruiters filter on key skills, notice period, current CTC and expected CTC. Fill key skills with the keyword bank above.
  • Update the profile (even one word) every few days; Naukri ranks recently-updated profiles higher in recruiter search.
  • Notice period: if you are serving notice or your company allows buyout, say so; "Immediate / 15 days" profiles get many more calls. Do not misstate it.
  • Expected CTC is visible to recruiters. Enter your target range floor (e.g. 35L), not your current pay plus 30%. See Negotiation.

Instahyre, Cutshort, Wellfound

These are curated and skew to product companies and funded startups. Complete the profile once, mark yourself actively looking for a fixed window (say 8 weeks), and reply quickly; inactive responders get deprioritised.

09Common mistakes

These get resumes rejected or interviews derailed
  • Listing technologies you cannot defend (Cassandra, Go, Kubernetes operators) and then failing the first follow-up.
  • Bullets that describe the product, not your work: "e-signing platform used by top banks" is context, not an achievement.
  • Fake-precise metrics ("improved performance by 73.4%") with no baseline you can explain.
  • Two pages of every Jira ticket since 2020; no service ownership visible.
  • Skill bars and star ratings ("Java ★★★★☆"). They mean nothing and break ATS parsing.
  • Confidential details: client names, internal hostnames, exact revenue. Use ranges and "a top-5 private bank".
  • Old stack prominence: Struts/JSP or a 2019 college project above your current service ownership.
  • Same resume for every role. For AI-engineer roles, move the AI section higher; for payments roles, lead with idempotency and consistency work.

10Final checklist

  • One page, single column, text PDF, ATS paste test passed.
  • Each owned service has: what it does, a scale number, a decision with a rejected alternative, a measured impact.
  • Every bullet starts with an ownership verb; no "worked on / helped / responsible for".
  • Every number has a baseline you can explain and a source you can name (dashboard, cost report).
  • At least one mentoring/leadership bullet and one incident/reliability bullet.
  • AI work stated with evaluation and cost, not adjectives.
  • Skills section contains only things you can be grilled on for 10 minutes.
  • LinkedIn headline, Open-to-Work (recruiters only), Naukri key skills and notice period updated.
  • For each bullet you have prepared the follow-up: "why that, what else did you consider, what would you change?"
Should I mention my current CTC or notice period on the resume?

No. Neither belongs on the resume. Notice period goes on Naukri/Instahyre profile fields and in the first recruiter call. CTC is discussed only on calls, and ideally anchored on your expected range, not the current figure.

Do I need a GitHub link?

Only if it shows something an interviewer would read: a small RAG service, a rate-limiter library with tests, a merged PR to a known project. An empty or tutorial-only GitHub is neutral at best. Your capstone from this playbook is a good candidate.

How do I show a promotion within the same company?

One company heading, two title lines with dates ("Senior Software Engineer, Jul 2024 – Present / Software Engineer, Jul 2021 – Jun 2024"). Promotions are strong evidence of level; make them visible.
